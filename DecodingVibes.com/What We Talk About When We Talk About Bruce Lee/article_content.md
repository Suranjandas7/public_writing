Bruce Lee does not qualify as an academic philosopher

Bruce Lee attended the University of Washington, and it is often cited that he “studied philosophy” there. 

However, what they often don’t tell you is that his actual major was drama and that he took a few philosophy classes. He would work as a waiter and teach martial arts to students on the side to pay his way through college, but would eventually drop out to pursue a career in acting and filmmaking. 

From a strictly academic perspective, Bruce Lee does not stand a chance of being considered to have made any meaningful contributions to philosophy. While you can easily find numerous citations of work by contemporary thinkers like Thomas Nagel in academia, Lee never wrote an academic paper. So it is clear that he wasn’t a philosopher in the strictly academic sense. We do not need to look very deeply to understand why this is the case.

However, let’s not be boring and stop here. Just because someone has not studied philosophy in an academic setting does not inherently disqualify them from thinking about and sharing their thoughts on life, existence, and the human condition.

And Mr. Lee did speak about it a lot. So, in that sense, there is a body of work to examine. We can look at what he said, what he followed, and the life he led, and still answer the question that is the thesis of this article: what was Bruce Lee’s philosophy, and does it actually make any sense? And despite it making or not making sense, why is the image still so powerful today? 

The difference between art and philosophy

To continue with this article, it is important to establish baseline definitions of two terms: art and philosophy, and how they differ. It is okay to skip this section entirely, which provides only ancillary context.

A very simple but all-encompassing definition of art (to me) is something that can be performed or experienced, and that triggers a certain emotion, thought, or feeling in the audience. 

A horror movie seeks to trigger fear in you; a love song seeks to trigger the emotional, loving feeling in you; a public awareness campaign seeks to trigger thoughts about a particular issue; and so on. 

Additionally, art usually has some sort of method and practice to it - for example, a filmmaker needs to understand cinematography, scriptwriting, and editing, while a painter needs to learn about colors, composition, the actual method of painting, and so on.

So yes, almost anything can be art, just not good or enjoyable art. For example, [insert a note about the guy who turned the toilet into an art installation]. The reason I ramble is to make it clear that martial arts is definitely an art, and that Bruce Lee mastered it. 

On the other hand, philosophy cannot be about just anything.

The objective of any philosophical work is not to trigger emotions or feelings (although it can be known to do so). But rather, it is concerned with logic. The intention might not be to make sense of the world, but every philosophical idea must stand scrutiny and be logically wrestled with. There are also many tough questions to which there isn’t really a “correct” answer. The job of philosophy here is to provide a logical framework that can provide a potential answer to the questions.

Calling art stupid or illogical is not a valid argument because art need not be grounded in firm logic to be effective in its core objective. But calling a philosophy stupid or illogical is a completely valid argument if you can prove it, since philosophy has to be grounded in firm logic and be ready to defend itself against hostile questions.

Bruce Lee once said on the subject of Jeet Kune Do, his specific style of martial arts, “Jeet Kune Do is not an organized institution that one can be a member of. Either you understand or you don't, and that is that.” 

As a philosophy, it fails the moment you make a claim like that. The power of philosophy and ideas is that they can stand scrutiny on their own, not on how well you associate with them. If that cop-out were available to most philosophers, the books wouldn’t be nearly as boring and heavy.

What is Jeet Kune Do, or the way of the intercepting fist 

Of course, Bruce Lee had developed his own fighting system. 

Instead of following the rigid technical standards most martial arts demanded, he developed a style based purely on pragmatism. The famous “be like water” quote is a direct reference to this system. His point, within this particular context, was to be extremely flexible and not get lost within the mythology and aesthetics of the practice. Take what is useful and disregard what is not.

The style would eventually be known as Jeet Kune Do or “the way of the intercepting fist.” Other maxims include a focus on balance and using motion effectively, and things like striking the enemy while the enemy is striking you (emphasis on the intercepting part). However, the important thing is that Bruce Lee never claimed this was a specific style. 

“I have not invented a "new style," composite, modified or otherwise that is set within distinct form as apart from "this" method or "that" method.” - Bruce Lee 

In fact, what Bruce Lee was teaching through his technique was to stop focusing on dogma and to avoid being tied to a single way of fighting. Instead, the student should seek to learn as many techniques as possible and derive their own form of the system.

So far, all of this is really great in the context of martial arts. Later in life, Bruce Lee would come to regard this method as a philosophy of life as well, but unfortunately, he did not live long enough to fulfill that objective.

After his death, the book “The Tao of Jeet Kune Do” was published in [year]. However, Bruce Lee did not write that book. The bulk of the content was found in notes he scribbled while recovering from a back injury that left him unable to practice. Almost all of it was scribbled in a single session. Other source material includes notes he kept on his fighting style.

He did want to write a book, but not one on philosophy or life in general. He wanted to write a specific book on martial arts and his approach to the art. The book was published posthumously by several editors who were given access and permission by his widow. 

It is one of the most cited books when we talk about his philosophy today. And it had almost nothing to do with him. Bruce Lee was a true martial artist, a visionary, but he was not, and did not want to be, a philosopher, nor did he want to engage with that field and its concerns at all in any academic sense. From my reading, it seems more like a Joe Rogan-esque approach to intellectual topics rather than a serious venture, as later marketing initiatives would attempt to portray it.

However, his being an exceptional martial artist was not enough for the world, leading to the image/meme that still effectively lives in our collective mindspace today, which the AI content creator is trying to exploit, and which Hollywood had successfully exploited to the point that there is a whole genre of such movies.

The man, the myth, and the legend

The Clones of Bruce Lee/Death Penalty on Three Robots was a 1980 movie, released seven years after his death. The plot of the movie centers on three clones of Bruce Lee who fight various villains with the overall goal of combating crime in Southeast Asia. The clones were played by notable actors in the Bruceploitation subgenre, which had become massively popular after Bruce Lee's death. 

Hong Kong’s movie industry went into a panic after the sudden passing, as Bruce Lee had become an international star with the release of Enter the Dragon. They had found a formula to break into the Western market, and they just lost the jewel and the guy who effectively made all this happen. So they went looking for replacements.
