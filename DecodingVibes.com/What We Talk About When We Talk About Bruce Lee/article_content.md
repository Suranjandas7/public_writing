I recently spent an entire afternoon watching YouTube reels of Bruce Lee performing martial arts in slow motion. In some of them, he’s doing really strong kicks while talking about how we should be like water; in others, he’s usually engaged in a very intense conversation, saying things directly to the camera or to someone else.

Only once the afternoon had fully passed, and I'd watched what felt like over thirty short videos, did I realize they were all (or at least the majority of them) completely AI-generated. 

When I asked Google Gemini 3.1 Pro Mode (known for its deep thinking abilities) about this, it gave me the typical “You’re not crazy” response, assuring me that it was becoming a pretty common tactic on YouTube these days to create such videos and waste the time of otherwise totally-employed and useful people like me, for views and dollars.

But okay, so what?

The reason why this felt like a teachable moment was not that I had been duped by AI videos. If generated properly, AI video is extremely good today, and within certain parameters (though it is still massively expensive for most use cases).

But rather the simple question rang in my mind - why Bruce Lee?

AI could have been used to generate anything, but whoever created those videos deliberately chose Bruce Lee as the subject. 

And the best part was that it totally worked! I was hooked on those videos. Bruce Lee felt like a mythical being, as if he were somehow interacting with the force on planet Earth. 

And this made me really think - where did the whole “Bruce Lee as a philosopher” meme emerge in the first place? Was there any truth to the idea, or was it just a caricature/helpful image propagated by media firms?

I wanted to find out if the idea of Bruce Lee that those AI-generated reels sold to me and what has arguably been sold by Hollywood for decades, of this mystical man from Asia who was amazing at martial arts and also a legit thinker, was actually true or just a marketing gimmick?

Who was Bruce Lee, the actual man, and what was the meme that his image and personal brand became completely attached to, and why does the idea of him (the meme of it all) still exist in the cultural zeitgeist today, and why does it still land so hard?

Bruce Lee and the philosophy of punching

From a strictly academic perspective, Bruce Lee does not stand a chance of being considered as having made any meaningful contributions to the field of philosophy. While you can easily find numerous citations of work by contemporary thinkers like Thomas Nagel in academia, nobody is really citing Bruce Lee.

So it is clear that he wasn’t a philosopher in the strictly academic sense. We do not need to look very deeply to understand why this is the case.

However, let’s not be boring and stop here. Just because someone has not studied philosophy in an academic setting does not inherently disqualify them from thinking about and sharing their thoughts on life, existence, and the human condition.

And Mr. Lee did speak about it a lot. So, in that sense, there is a body of work to examine. We can look at what he said, what he followed, and the life he led, and still answer the question that is the thesis of this article: what was Bruce Lee’s philosophy, and does it actually make any sense?

The difference between art and philosophy

However, before we get into the specifics, it is important to establish baseline definitions of two terms: art and philosophy, and how they differ. 

A very simple but all-encompassing definition of a piece of art (to me) is something that can be performed or experienced, and that triggers a certain emotion, thought, or feeling in the audience. 

A horror movie seeks to trigger fear in you; a love song seeks to trigger the emotional, loving feeling in you; a public awareness campaign seeks to trigger thoughts about a particular issue; and so on. 

Additionally, art usually has some sort of method to it - for example, a filmmaker needs to understand cinematography, scriptwriting, editing, while a painter needs to learn about colors, composition, the actual method of painting, and so on.

So anything can be art, just not good or enjoyable art. For example, [insert a note about the guy who turned the toilet into an art installation]. The reason I ramble is to make it clear that martial arts is definitely an art, and that Bruce Lee mastered it. 

On the other hand, philosophy cannot be anything.

The objective of any philosophical work is not to trigger emotions or feelings (although it can be known to do so). But rather, it is concerned with frameworks and testable or logical ideas that build upon themselves to provide something of value that helps us understand the world or ourselves.

Calling art stupid or illogical is not a valid argument, in my opinion, because art does not need to be grounded in firm logic to be effective in its core objective. But calling a philosophy stupid or illogical is a completely valid argument if you can prove it, since philosophy has to be grounded in firm logic and be ready to defend itself against hostile questions.

Bruce Lee once said on the subject of Jeet Kune Do, his specific style of martial arts, “Jeet Kune Do is not an organized institution that one can be a member of. Either you understand or you don't, and that is that.” As a philosophy, it fails the moment you make a claim like that. The power of philosophy and ideas is that they can stand scrutiny on their own, not on how well you associate with them. If that cop-out were available to most philosophers, the books wouldn’t be nearly as boring and heavy.
