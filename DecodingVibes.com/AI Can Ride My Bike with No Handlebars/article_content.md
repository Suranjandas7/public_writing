I don’t remember the first time I heard Flobot’s “Handlebars” ([https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3M104iSE3CI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3M104iSE3CI)), but I have been hearing it a lot again recently in an attempt to connect with who I was fifteen years ago. Even though it is pretty simple to understand, the lyrics do have a lot of substance. 

The lyrics of the song start with someone talking about being happy as they can ride a bicycle with no handlebars, and by the end, they threaten to end the world in a holocaust. There can be several interpretations of what any lyrics mean, but in this case, we can find a quote from the artist that clarifies the intended meaning:

\> "The song is about the idea that we have so much incredible potential as human beings to be destructive or to be creative. And it's tragic to me that the appetite for military innovation is endless, but when it comes to taking on a project like ending world hunger, it's seen as outlandish. It's not treated with the same seriousness… We have these little moments of creativity, these bursts of innovation, and every time that happens, that innovation is used to oppress and destroy people." 

MC Jonny 5 / Jamie Laurie (main singer and founder of Flobots) to MTV News ([https://genius.com/Flobots-handlebars-lyrics](https://genius.com/Flobots-handlebars-lyrics))

The song speaks about how scale almost always corrupts human innovation; that the same core innovation spirit in us that lets us find meaning and joy in simple acts like riding a bicycle with no handlebars can bring us meaning (and in some cases, joy) of guiding a missile with no satellite; that the human ego at scale is a dangerous thing.

## AGI, Consciousness and the AI Arms Race

\> I think AI will probably, like, most likely sort of lead to the end of the world, but in the meantime, there will be great companies created with serious machine learning.

Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, [https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YE5adUeTe\_I](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YE5adUeTe_I)

The idea that machines will rise up and take over the world is not new. Many sci-fi works explore the situation, but most involve a hyper-intelligent system that is either fully conscious as a being or can act as if it is. Most of them will not explain how it works, in the sense of how these computers or software turned into this thing. 

The thing just shows up buck naked in a bar with a German accent and demands your clothes. It doesn’t tell you, “Oh yes, I understand what you’re saying. Yes, please sit down while I give you a presentation going through everything, all the scientific progress we made, how the machines actually work, what is consciousness and how we manipulate it, and et cetera.” That would make for a very boring movie.

\> Once we build a generally intelligent system, that basically we will ask it to figure out a way to make an investment return for you.

Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, [https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/18/sam-altmans-leap-of-faith/](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/18/sam-altmans-leap-of-faith/)

Consciousness is a subject that always seems to be at the center of the AI discussion but is never discussed seriously. For example, a tech CEO might boldly proclaim that their company will reach AGI (in fact, a specific form of conscious AGI) in the next three years. However, instead of following through on the legal, ethical, and philosophical framework of the company owning conscious beings, they would move on to how their ad business did 5% more year-over-year. It is almost as if they don’t take the conscious AI claims seriously; one would assume.

But is consciousness required or a part of the definition of AGI? The consensus seems to say no.

And in recent years, we’ve also seen some pullback on the rhetoric and leaders trying to define AGI as a capacity threshold rather than a claim of consciousness. For example, last year Sam Altman said: “I think it’s not a super useful term” ([https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/11/sam-altman-says-agi-is-a-pointless-term-experts-agree.html](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/11/sam-altman-says-agi-is-a-pointless-term-experts-agree.html))  when talking about AGI. 

However, while a non-conscious AI system that can perform human tasks at a superlative capacity will mean you don’t need to worry about ethical and moral issues of deploying the said AI, it would still be a dangerous thing, just not Skynet.

In his essay “The Adolescence of Technology” ([https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology)), Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei proclaims seriously, “humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it is deeply unclear whether we possess the maturity to wield it.” Most of the essay seems to be an argument for more regulations on AI; you can read it at your own risk.

Either way, AGI or no AGI, we can assume that if the AI arms race goes well, then in the worst case (from the point of view of AI companies) is that LLMs do not gain consciousness with more compute, but with better harnesses and integration, AI can become genuinely useful in every field to the point where the “controllers” of AI, i.e. the companies that control them, will be given an almost unimaginable power and it is deeply unclear if they have the maturity to wield it.

At that point, the worries expressed by Mr. Amodei in his essay become realistic. However, if his answer can be found in the blog post that starts with “There is a scene in the movie version of Carl Sagan’s book Contact where the main character, an astronomer…”, I deem it not serious enough. Even on a rudimentary reading, Mr. Amodei skips most of the difficult questions and focuses on things like billionaire philanthropy and preventing hardware access to oh-so-evil China. 

This brings me back to handlebars, and these particular lines:

\> I can hand out a million vaccinations / Or let 'em all die in exasperation / Have 'em all healed from their lacerations / Or have 'em all killed by assassination / I can make anybody go to prison / Just because I don't like 'em / And I can do anything with no permission

A world where that stands true for private US companies like Google, OpenAI, Amazon, Anthropic, or any private company does not sound like a good world. And it might be the world we are heading towards, AGI or no AGI. 

## The Ego Remains the Problem

It is a bit funny how tech CEOs will often claim that artificial intelligence, “the AI”, will take away x percent of jobs in y years. But they never say that their company will do it; they never say they’re investing billions hoping this happens, but rather “the AI” will take away the jobs and replace them. 

Yet, from what we can see, there is no difference between OpenAI and the models it runs behind its closed doors, and the same for every private frontier AI lab. But there seems to be a sense that it’s okay to attribute agency to the AI when it's convenient while you own the shares and all financial gains. You blame the same demon you command and profit from. 

If an AI system is not conscious (and let’s assume it cannot be at the time being and in the near future unless proven otherwise), but is very, very good at human-level tasks, then whoever controls that system will gain massive power and influence. But it will still be a human or a group of egos.

So the moral problem here isn’t really whether AI can be conscious or how to live in a world where that is true. But rather, should we trust in AI labs to be worthy of wielding such power? History tells us that no private entity is worthy of that power.
